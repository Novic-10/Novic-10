Executive Summary & Overview

In a critical race against time, law enforcement initiated an investigation into a potential bomb threat targeting NYC. Information suggested a gang of attackers had entered the city, planning to detonate an explosive device. As part of the investigation, a suspect named Zerry was detained, and his laptop was seized as digital evidence.

The device was sent to the NYC digital forensics division for analysis. The primary objective was to uncover any information related to the potential attack, including details about the explosive device, planned date and time, and its intended location, to determine if the threat was credible or a hoax. This writeup details the forensic examination of Zerry's laptop to answer these urgent questions.

Key Takeaways & Security Insights

  • •
    Forensic Image Verification: The sector count and MD5 hash are crucial for verifying the integrity and completeness of a forensic image. This ensures that the acquired image matches the physical disk's capacity and that no data was lost or altered during the acquisition process, which is fundamental for maintaining the chain of custody and admissibility of evidence.
  • •
    Encrypted Messenger Analysis: Modern digital forensics often involves decrypting and analyzing data from encrypted communication applications like Signal. Locating configuration files and decryption keys is a critical step to access chat histories, attachments, and user profiles, which can contain vital intelligence.
  • •
    Metadata and Chat Content Importance: Even seemingly innocuous details like filenames, email addresses, and GPS coordinates embedded within chat messages or attachments can provide crucial intelligence for threat assessment, identifying targets, and understanding attack methodologies.
