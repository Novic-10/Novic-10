Executive Summary & Overview

This writeup details the investigation into a Business Email Compromise (BEC) attack targeting an organization. The compromise originated from a phishing email, leading to unauthorized access to the mailbox of Becky Lorray. The attacker, operating from multiple IP addresses, established persistence by creating malicious inbox rules designed to forward sensitive emails to an external address (Sabastian@flanaganspensions.co.uk) and delete legitimate communications. The ultimate goal of the attack was to initiate fraudulent financial transactions, specifically a fund transfer to the FIRST BANK OF NIGERIA LTD. This analysis leverages Azure AD logs and email artifacts to reconstruct the attacker's timeline and identify key indicators of compromise.

Question & Answer Solutions

1. What is the source address of the initial phishing email?

Sabastian@flanaganspensions.co.uk

Reasoning: Identified from the email logs as the sender of the initial malicious communication.

2. What type of compromise is this?

BEC

Reasoning: The nature of the attack, involving email account compromise for financial fraud, aligns with Business Email Compromise (BEC).

3. What are the two IPs utilised by the TA?

159.203.17.81, 95.181.232.30

Reasoning: The first IP, 159.203.17.81, was found in Azure CSV logs associated with UserLoggedIN operations. The second IP, 95.181.232.30, was linked to the creation of a new-inboxrule operation, both identified via the ClientIp field.

4. Which bank is the transaction sent to?

FIRST BANK OF NIGERIA LTD

Reasoning: Analysis of the email containing SWIFT information revealed the beneficiary bank as FIRST BANK OF NIGERIA LTD, a Nigerian bank.

5. What is the name of the Inbox folder created during the compromise?

History

Reasoning: Identified by filtering for the FolderCreated operation code in the logs.

6. What word is the first inbox rule created looking for, within the subject or body, prior to deleting the email?

Withdrawal

Reasoning: Examination of the details of the first malicious inbox rule revealed it was configured to filter for the keyword Withdrawal in email subjects or bodies.

Key Takeaways & Security Insights

  • •
    Correlation of Events: Always correlate the first malicious action discovered with preceding login times and activities. This helps in pinpointing the exact moment of compromise and the initial access vector.
  • •
    Understanding Operation Codes: Familiarity with various log operation codes (e.g., UserLoggedIN, new-inboxrule, FolderCreated) is crucial for parsing log information and understanding the specific actions taken by an attacker.
  • •
    Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Strong MFA policies are essential to prevent unauthorized access, even if credentials are compromised via phishing.
  • •
    Email Security Awareness: Regular training for employees on identifying phishing attempts and suspicious emails can significantly reduce the risk of initial compromise.
