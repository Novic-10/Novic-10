Cyber Incident Writeup

Business Email Compromise (BEC) Investigation

#CyberchefCTF #BECCTF #EmailAnalysisCTF #TimeLineExplorerCTF

Executive Summary

This report details the investigation into a suspected cyber incident involving unauthorized bank transfers from the company pension fund. The incident has been identified as a Business Email Compromise (BEC), where an attacker gained unauthorized access to an employee's email account, specifically Becky Lorray's, to initiate fraudulent financial transactions. The attacker utilized sophisticated techniques including creating inbox rules to forward and delete emails, and impersonating legitimate entities to facilitate the transfer of funds to an external Nigerian bank. Our analysis focuses on identifying the attacker's methods, infrastructure, and the scope of the compromise.

Challenge Overview

The objective of this Capture The Flag (CTF) challenge was to act as an incident responder, analyzing provided logs and artifacts to understand the full scope of a cyber incident. The primary goal was to determine how the compromise occurred, identify key attacker indicators, and trace the fraudulent financial activity. This involved parsing email logs, identifying suspicious login activities, analyzing email rules, and tracing bank transfer details to answer specific investigative questions.

Key Takeaways & Detection/Security Insights

  • Correlation is Key: Once a malicious action is discovered (e.g., `New-InboxRule`), it is crucial to correlate it with preceding events, especially login times and source IPs, to establish the attacker's initial access point and timeline.
  • Understanding Operation Codes: Familiarity with common log operation codes (e.g., `UserLoggedIn`, `New-InboxRule`, `FolderCreated`, `DeleteMessage`, `SMTPSend`) is fundamental for parsing log information and understanding attacker actions.
  • Monitoring Inbox Rule Creation: The creation of new inbox rules, especially those involving forwarding to external addresses or deleting messages, is a strong indicator of compromise and should trigger immediate alerts.
  • Geolocation & IP Reputation: Suspicious logins from unusual geographic locations or IPs with poor reputation scores should be flagged. In this case, IPs like 95.181.232.30 were associated with attacker activity.
  • Financial Transaction Verification: All requests for significant financial transactions, particularly those involving changes to beneficiary details or international transfers, must undergo stringent multi-factor verification processes outside of email communication.
  • Phishing Awareness: The initial compromise likely stemmed from a phishing email, highlighting the ongoing need for robust employee training and advanced email security solutions.

Step-by-Step Investigation Workflow

  1. Initial Log Review: Began by examining Azure CSV logs for suspicious activities, focusing on user login events and email-related operations.
  2. Identify Suspicious Logins: Located `UserLoggedIn` operation codes and identified unusual `ClientIp` addresses, noting failed login attempts followed by successful ones from the same suspicious IPs.
  3. Trace Attacker Actions: Correlated suspicious login times with subsequent email operations. Specifically looked for `New-InboxRule` and `FolderCreated` operation codes to understand how the attacker maintained persistence and exfiltrated information.
  4. Analyze Inbox Rules: Investigated the details of newly created inbox rules to determine their purpose, such as forwarding emails to external addresses (95.181.232.30:23701) or deleting specific messages.
  5. Examine Email Content: Reviewed emails, particularly those related to financial transactions, to extract critical information like SWIFT/BIC codes and beneficiary bank details.
  6. Identify Phishing Source: Determined the source address of the initial phishing email that likely led to the account compromise.
  7. Document Indicators of Compromise (IOCs): Compiled a list of identified IPs, email addresses, and timestamps associated with malicious activity.
